book report

Name of the book: Foundation
[middle]
(4/5 stars)
Overall, it's a good science fiction book, made of many short stories with long time gaps between them (a bit like a science-fiction version of The Silmarillion). I consider that review somewhat partial, since I read only the first book in the trilogy of "The Foundation Series", and the majority of the plot has not yet concluded. My main criticism of this is that for a book whose main theme is the repetition of historical events (specifically, the Middle Ages), I personally think that writing about past events and drawing obvious relations to the present makes a greater effect than writing about theoretical future events. With that said, I really like the idea of a wide book that talks about many events, sometimes with a chronological period between each story.
[top left]
Connection between the book and the world.
The book ties some obvious connections to the real world. Most notably (and as I later discovered, Asimov himself said that), the Middle Ages that began after the fall of the Roman Empire required humanity to rediscover its own technological breakthroughs from the Greeks and Romans once the Renaissance began, versus the "Dark Ages" that started after the fall of the Galactic Empire in the book that are characterized by a decrease in technology.
[top right]
Could the story take place as it is on a deserted island? Why or why not?
The story could not have taken place on a deserted island. First of all, one of the main ideas of the book -- the science of Psychohistory -- is based on statistical analysis of an insanely large group of people (in contrast to a small population of a deserted island). In addition, it requires having giant Empires and Kingdoms that span great distances that could not have existed on a small island (and arguably not even on the entire earth).
[bottom left]
If I could give one gift to the main character, what would it be?
As the story consists of several short stories that span a long period of time, there is no "main character". Though if I had to declare someone as the "main character", it would be Hari Seldon: the inventor of Psychohistory, who "orchestrated" and predicted all of the events that happened in the book long after his death. If you could have given him one gift, it would be the knowledge of whether his plan to shorten the dark age that is expected to follow the fall of the Galactic Empire. I don't know how to answer this question, since the story resolves in future books which I've yet to read.
